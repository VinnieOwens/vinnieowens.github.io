Heisman Analytics

Played for vs Drafted by

The franchise chart has its own toggle, separate from the amber and blue one. It asks the same question two ways. Played for counts winners who had at least one season with a franchise. Drafted by counts winners the franchise selected in the NFL, AFL or AAFC draft, whether or not they ever played there. The bars, the title and the footnote all switch together, and the chart always shows all 32 teams in their team colors.

  • Raiders: 12 winners played for them, but they only drafted 5. The gap is winners who got there after starting somewhere else.
  • Lions: the most picks (10), and 11 winners played there, so they barely move between views.
  • Texans, Dolphins, Seahawks: none has drafted a Heisman winner (0 in this view), yet winners have played for all three.
  • Both views cross-highlight: click a college or position and the bars show just that group, in either mode.

What the data says

A few things the report surfaced that I didn't expect to find.

13 of 90

Never logged an NFL season

The trophy isn't a pro-career guarantee. Ten never played pro football anywhere, and three more only played outside the NFL.

40 → 21

The position flipped

Running backs won 40 of the first 64 awards (1935–1999). Quarterbacks have won 21 of the last 26 (2000–2025).

11.1 seasons

The 1980s class lasted longest

Winners from the 1980s averaged 11.1 NFL seasons, the peak of any decade. The 2020s average of 3.2 is low because most of those careers are still going.

21 seasons

The longest careers are a tie

Vinny Testaverde played 21 NFL seasons. Counting every league, Doug Flutie matches him at 21 pro seasons.

12 vs 5

Where they play isn't always where they're picked

Flip the franchise chart from Played for to Drafted by and the Raiders fall from 12 winners to 5. The Lions barely move: the most picks (10), with 11 who suited up.

90% vs 81%

Quarterbacks make it a bit more often

35 of 39 QBs got an NFL season, against 35 of 43 running backs. Same count, different base.

How it's built

The interesting part isn't the charts, it's the model and measures underneath them.

1. SourceSix CSVs: seasons, team colors, franchises, positions, drafts
2. Power QueryImported as typed tables, kept as plain CSVs on purpose
3. ModelSeason facts plus lookup tables, and calculated tables for summaries and toggles
4. DAXMeasures for the toggles, counts, legend dimming and chart sorting
5. ReportMatrices, button slicers, cross-highlighting

Why a matrix and not a Gantt chart

The obvious tool for "career timelines" is a Gantt visual, and I started there. The third-party options had account walls, hidden settings and a look I couldn't configure. So I restructured the data to one row per player per season and built it with a native matrix and conditional formatting: each cell's background color comes from the team's hex code through conditional formatting. That also made the legend and the cross-highlighting possible.

The franchise chart with its own toggle

One chart, two meanings. A small disconnected table (Franchise Basis) feeds a button slicer, and a single measure switches between counting players who played for a franchise and players it drafted, applying the page's current selection through TREATAS so it still cross-highlights.

Franchise Basis = DATATABLE("Basis", STRING, {{"Played for"}, {"Drafted by"}})

Franchise Winners =
VAR F = SELECTEDVALUE('nfl_franchises'[Franchise])
VAR Basis = SELECTEDVALUE('Franchise Basis'[Basis], "Played for")
VAR Played =
    CALCULATE(
        DISTINCTCOUNT('heisman_winners_by_year'[Name]),
        'heisman_winners_by_year'[Level] = "Pro",
        'heisman_winners_by_year'[Franchise] = F,
        TREATAS(VALUES(Players[Name]), 'heisman_winners_by_year'[Name]))
VAR Drafted =
    CALCULATE(
        DISTINCTCOUNT('heisman_drafts'[Name]),
        'heisman_drafts'[Franchise] = F,
        TREATAS(VALUES(Players[Name]), 'heisman_drafts'[Name]))
RETURN COALESCE(IF(Basis = "Drafted by", Drafted, Played), 0)

Legend that dims only when you've narrowed the selection

The legend colors are a measure too. With nothing selected, every team shows its real color. Once the report is narrowed to a player or group, teams nobody in the selection played for fade out.

LegendColor =
VAR PlayersNow = DISTINCTCOUNT('heisman_winners_by_year'[Name])
VAR PlayersAll = CALCULATE(DISTINCTCOUNT('heisman_winners_by_year'[Name]),
                           ALL('heisman_winners_by_year'))
VAR Narrowed = PlayersNow < PlayersAll
VAR Used =
    CALCULATE(COUNTROWS('heisman_winners_by_year'),
        TREATAS(VALUES('team_colors'[Team]), 'heisman_winners_by_year'[Team])) > 0
RETURN IF(NOT Narrowed || Used,
          SELECTEDVALUE('team_colors'[Hex]),
          SELECTEDVALUE('team_colors'[Faded_Hex]))

A small fix I'm oddly proud of

I wanted the position pie ordered so the tiny slices wouldn't collide and hide their labels. Power BI's normal answer (sort by a column) threw a circular dependency error, because the order column lived in the same calculated table. The workaround: a measure dropped into the visual's Tooltips well, which makes it available as a Sort axis option.

Slice Order =
SWITCH(SELECTEDVALUE(Players[Position]),
    "DB", 1, "RB", 2, "Multi", 3, "QB", 4, "WR", 5, 99)

Smaller details

  • Relocated teams: a calculated column maps era-specific names (Oakland, Los Angeles and Las Vegas Raiders, the Oilers and Titans and so on) to one legend entry.
  • Toggle-aware KPIs: the cards, chart titles and bar colors all read one Mode Is NFL measure, so the page can't show amber numbers under a blue title.
  • Where cross-highlighting stops: it only works between visuals on the same page that measure the same table. I built a single Players summary table so the whole Insights page shares one.

The data

Every season of every career is a judgment call, so the rules are written down.

The six tables
FileWhat it holds
heisman_winners_by_year.csvOne row per player per season: team, level (College or Pro), year. 937 rows.
team_colors.csv110 teams with a primary color, a category and legend colors.
franchises.csvMaps each historical team name to its modern franchise.
nfl_franchises.csvThe 32 current franchises with colors. Drives the franchise chart.
heisman_positions.csvEach winner's position in the Heisman season.
heisman_drafts.csvEvery pro draft selection: 99 picks covering 87 of the 90 winners.
Rules for what counts as a season
  • One team per player per season. A midseason trade goes to the team they ended on.
  • Military service, redshirt, transfer sit-out and freshman-squad years are left blank.
  • Injured and suspended seasons count only if the player was still on the roster (injured reserve and so on).
  • Retirements, preseason cuts and camp-only stints are blank.
  • Included leagues: NFL, AFL, AAFC, WFL, USFL, CFL and NFL Europe. Excluded: XFL, UFL, AAF and FCF.
  • "Seasons played" counts seasons with a team, not the span of a career.
  • Drafted by counts every NFL, AFL, AAFC and supplemental draft pick, played or not. A winner picked by two teams counts for both, so the bars total more than the number of players.
Sources and checking

The career data was compiled with AI assistance (Claude), then checked player by player against each winner's English Wikipedia page, with Pro-Football-Reference and cflapedia as a second source for doubtful cases. That check corrected the pro careers of 33 players and the college years of 18. Draft picks come from Wikipedia's list of Heisman winners and were cross-checked against NFL.com. The full change log is in the repo.

Read the verification log

Known limitations
  • Wikipedia is the main source, so errors on those pages carry through. Where pages disagree, the seasons follow the varsity record.
  • The 2026 season is in progress, so active players' totals and the 2020s averages will keep growing.
  • Team colors are primary colors picked for legibility, not official brand specs.
