How It Works

  1. 1) Source the data

    Liquipedia holds a record of every team's placement and roster at every TI.

  2. 2) Scrape and parse the data

    From this, a dataset is constructed of every player to ever play at TI, along with where they finished, what team they were on at the time, and what that team's logo was at the time.

  3. 3) Scoring, part 1

    Using a geometric decay formula, we assign a 1st place win a score of 100 points. A second place win is worth 0.7 × 100. A third place win is worth 0.7 × 0.7 × 100. This pattern continues.

  4. 4) Scoring, part 2

    Players should be rewarded for longevity in the professional scene, but not overly so. To solve for this, order their scores from each of their TI showings from best to worst. Then, use a second geometric decay formula, this one with a decay rate of 0.5 rather than 0.7. Their best placement is worth 100% of its points. Their next best score is worth 0.5 × 100% of its score. Their third best showing is worth 0.5 × 0.5 × 100% of its value. The pattern continues.

  5. 5) Clean up the player pool

    A total of 470 players have competed at TI, but not all of them had a substantial enough impact to have name recognition. Trim the player pool down to keep the game full of recognizable players, ending with a total player pool of 308. Also, some players have changed their names over the years — make sure their names are up-to-date.

  6. 6) Assign rarity tiers

    Rank players against others in their own role, then sort into 5 tiers, based on their score percentile: Legendary, Epic, Rare, Uncommon, Common.

  7. 7) Build the drafting system

    Being offered a Legendary should be rare, but not vanishingly so. Playtesting resulted in the values the game runs off of now, which "feel right." Make sure the player isn't offered a role they already have filled.

  8. 8) Score the built team

    A simple sum of player scores, and then a rating based on the range that outcome fits into.
